Read 2026-11-16 with Playwright Chromium (screenshots in media/blog/virtual-makeup-try-on-lighting/research/, git-ignored). Google blocks automation, so result lists came from WebSearch.

Gap we fill: one page that covers photo setup, why light and screens shift a shade (with a Kelvin table), undertone and finish (table), a step order ending in a real swatch, lip vs eye vs brow tips, a fair comparison of testing methods, and honest limits.

## Sources used (all opened and read)

- PhotoWorkout, Color Temperature in Photography: Kelvin values by light source (approximate), skin looks jaundiced if too warm and ill if too cool, mixed light cannot be fixed with one white balance, AWB may shift between frames, iPhone WB lock by tap and hold.
- ExpertPhotography, white balance: tungsten gives an almost orange cast, AWB struggles under tungsten.
- Apple Support (Mac), display setup: True Tone shifts color temperature continuously; Night Shift warm shift means colors "won't accurately reflect your content's true values".
- Healthline, skin undertones: warm, cool, neutral; undertone differs from surface tone; vein, jewelry, neutral clothing clues; personal preference matters most.
- Ulta, foundation undertone: swatch two to four shades at similar depth, jaw to neck, natural daylight, wait for dry down; "helpful starting points, not strict rules"; shade names vary by brand; digital shade images are a guide only.
- Woman & Home (Emily Simms, No7): vein and jewelry clues and lip shade families per undertone.
- Times LIVE: lipstick true to life, shimmer and pearl shown poorly, blush hard to read on darker skin, no foundation matching, use good light and bare face.
- Glow Vault: vivid modes boost saturation, blue-light filters add warm cast, beauty filters on by default, bold colors bounce onto skin and lips, compare a known lipstick with the screen.
- Perfect Corp: retailers should compare digitized shades with the physical product under several lighting conditions.

## Unverified or deliberately left out

- No independent, quantitative accuracy study of makeup try on tools was found. The post says so and claims no accuracy figure.
- Retailer return policies: Sephora and Bobbi Brown pages blocked the browser. The post only tells readers to read the policy and states we did not check any.
- AnandTech True Tone measurements were not read (page returned the forum), so not used.
- Kelvin values vary by source (approximate); the post says so. The "what it does to a shade" column is labeled as our reading.
- Product facts used: 5 credits per result, 10 free credits (two results) for a new account, $10 monthly for 200 credits.
