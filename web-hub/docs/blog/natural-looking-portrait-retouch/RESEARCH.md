## Competitor gaps and information-gain plan

1. Nobody gives one table that pairs each retouch step with what to adjust and when to stop. We add it.
2. Nobody compares phone apps, Lightroom or Photoshop, and AI retouch tools fairly with cost and limits. We add a table with only verified facts and "price not confirmed" where unverified.
3. Over-edit signs are scattered as bullet lists. We pair each sign with why it happens and the fix.
4. A short, repeatable before/after check (100 percent zoom, thumbnail size, flip, wait, compare with the original).
5. Honesty and disclosure: peer-reviewed study on manipulated selfies, UK advertising rules, Norway's labeling rule, none of which the competitors cover.
6. Frequency separation explained in plain words from Adobe's own page, with the honest note that it is manual and easy to overdo, and that a gentle tool can do the same job for most people.

## Verified facts (with URLs, accessed 2026-11-16)

- Adobe, Photoshop frequency separation page (https://www.adobe.com/products/photoshop/frequency-separation): "Frequency separation is a technique used to separate color information from the other details of your image." High-frequency layer = fine details such as hair, texture, pores; low-frequency = shadows, tone, color. Adobe's action blurs the bottom layer with Gaussian Blur 5 px, subtracts it from the top layer (Apply Image, Subtract, Scale 2, Offset 128) and sets the top layer to Linear Light. Adobe says "frequency separation can take a lot of steps" and you "don't need to remove every feature of a person".
- Kleemans, Daalmans, Carbaat, Anschutz (2018), Media Psychology 21(1), 93-110, DOI 10.1080/15213269.2016.1257392 (https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15213269.2016.1257392): 144 girls aged 14 to 18 randomly saw original or manipulated (retouched and reshaped) Instagram selfies. Manipulated photos led to lower body image, especially in girls with higher social comparison tendency. Manipulated photos were rated more positively. Filters were noticed but reshaping of bodies was not noticed very well. Girls in both groups found the pictures realistic. Authors note limits (the manipulation might be too subtle to detect; more research needed).
- ASA blog "Why real beats fake", 1 May 2018 (https://www.asa.org.uk/news/why-real-beats-fake.html): advertisers may show products in the best light but must not exaggerate through digital post-production what a product can achieve; advertisers must show they have not altered images in a misleading way; L'Oreal ad banned 2011, Rimmel TV ad banned 2017. No blanket disclosure rule is stated.
- Norwegian Consumer Authority guideline (https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/english/the-norwegian-consumer-authoritys-guideline-on-labelling-retouched-advertising): advertising published from 1 July 2022 must be labeled when a person's body, face or skin is altered, including filters; even removing a single pimple triggers it; applies to advertisers and others who create advertising, including paid influencers; label covers about 7 percent of the image, upper left; exceptions include brightness or contrast adjustments, changes made before shooting (makeup, lighting) and color changes to hair, teeth or eyes. Applies to advertising, not private photos.
- Google Play listings (read 2026-11-16): Snapseed, Google LLC, "30+ pro tools and filters - no subscriptions, purchases, ads, or watermarks", has Healing and Selective tools. Adobe Lightroom (com.adobe.lrmobile): described as a free photo and video editor, lists In-app purchases, has Generative Remove. Facetune (Lightricks): contains ads and in-app purchases; lists skin tone and texture softening.
- Photography Icon retouching guide (https://photographyicon.com/photo-retouching/): suggests dodge and burn on a 50 percent gray Soft Light layer with a soft brush at 3 to 10 percent opacity, and lightening eye whites by 10 to 15 percent, not 50 percent; zoom out to actual viewing size to see if a change is visible. Vendor-neutral photography education site; treated as one retoucher's guidance, not a standard.
- Rangefinder (https://rangefinderonline.com/news-features/tips-techniques/the-beauty-behind-natural-retouching/): photographer Daryna Barykina says natural retouching can take longer, means choosing "between the major imperfections you want to remove and minor natural flaws you want to keep"; separation values depend on distance to subject.
